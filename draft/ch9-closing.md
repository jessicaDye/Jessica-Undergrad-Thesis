# Chapter 9: Closing: Augmentation, Not Replacement (outline)

*Outline drafted 19 Nov 2026. Target: about 1,200 words.*

**This is a lighter outline on purpose.** This chapter is your own argument about what the literature means, so instead of suggested topic sentences, it gives you the moves, prompts to write from, and the guardrails. It's the most personal chapter in the thesis; it should sound like you.

**Chapter claim (normative, and labeled as such):** keeping this expertise alive is what lets people check AI's work, so AI augments human ability instead of replacing it.

**Legend:** ✅ in `refs.bib` · ➕ add to `refs.bib`

## Guardrails

- **Mark the shift.** Open with a sentence that tells the reader this chapter moves from what the literature shows to what we should want. Chapters 2–8 stay descriptive; this one argues.
- **Pro-AI throughout.** The argument is not against AI. It's for the kind of AI use that keeps people able to step in.
- **Quote the encyclical from your printed copy.** Secondary sources point to ¶115, ¶118–130 and ¶172 for transhumanism and posthumanism. Check paragraph numbers and wording yourself. ➕ Add *Magnifica Humanitas* (Pope Leo XIV, signed 15 May 2026, published 25 May 2026) to `refs.bib`.
- Personal experience (using AI daily to learn) can appear as motivation, not as evidence.

## The moves, in order

**9.1 From "is" to "ought" (≈150 words)**
- Prompt: *What did chapters 2–8 establish, in two sentences, and what question does that leave that evidence alone can't answer?*

**9.2 The slide from "you'll need AI to compete" to dependence (≈300 words)**
- Prompt: *When does relying on a tool become being unable to work without it? What's the difference between using a calculator and not knowing arithmetic?*
- Connect to Bainbridge (✅ bainbridge1983) and the epistemic-debt result (➕): the risk isn't the tool; it's use that leaves people unable to repair what they own.

**9.3 Transhumanism, posthumanism and *Magnifica Humanitas* (≈450 words)**
- Prompt: *What does the encyclical say human dignity or human agency requires in relation to technology? Where does it welcome technology, and where does it draw a line?*
- Lay out the transhumanist hope (enhancement through technology) fairly before bringing in the encyclical's response.
- Prompt: *How does memory-model literacy, a very concrete skill, connect to the encyclical's broader concern? (One possible link: being able to verify a tool's work is a form of keeping agency over it.)*

**9.4 The pro-AI answer (≈200 words)**
- Prompt: *What would AI that augments look like in practice?* Use the evidence: AI that explains (Shen & Tamkin ✅, the explanation gate ➕), tools that keep a human reviewing patches (CodeMender ✅).
- Tie back to the spine: education and AI tool design should keep the specialist pipeline alive.

**9.5 Last word (≈100 words)**
- Return to the guiding question from ch. 1 and answer it in your own voice.
